# The ColorScheme is Light and Dark; Solar and Astral survive as Palette Presets

The **ColorScheme** keeps its name and loses its values: `Solar | Astral` becomes **`Light | Dark`**, across four stored surfaces and every living document. Solar and Astral survive as the first two **Palette Presets** — whole **Palettes**, one per ColorScheme, that Hexly ships ready to pick and that a **World Owner** copies into their **World Theme** as a starting point. Four more join them, two per ColorScheme.

The two halves are one decision. Renaming the axis to `Light | Dark` is only acceptable **because** the names survive: the evocative pair is the identity ADR-0006 was written to protect, and a rename that simply deleted them would trade a real thing for a legible one. Conversely, Palette Presets are only worth building **because** the rename creates a place to put the names. Recording them apart would make the argument in neither.

This **amends ADR-0075's Vocabulary bullet**, which said in so many words that "the day/night axis is a **ColorScheme** (`Solar` | `Astral`)". It does not reopen ADR-0006: one identity told at two hours of the day is still what Hexly wears, and is now what one _pair_ of Presets is for.

## What forced it

ADR-0075 renamed the axis from `theme` to `ColorScheme` and, in the same move, gave it evocative values. The concept name was the fix; the values were the cost, and eighteen months of prose have made the cost legible:

- **The two words carry no direction.** Nothing in "Astral" says _dark_. Every reader of the codebase — and every reader of `user-menu.component.ts` — has to learn a lookup, which is why `en.json` renders the toggle as **"Solar (light)"** and **"Astral (dark)"**: the copy translates the vocabulary back for the user at the point of use. A label that has to gloss itself is a label that failed.
- **The values are not the concept's to hold.** `Solar` and `Astral` name _specific colours_ — warm ivory paper and heliograph gold, midnight indigo and constellation gold. A World Owner authoring a crimson-and-charcoal World is still, in the schema, filling in a field called `astral`. That is the exact lie ADR-0075 condemned in `--color-gold` and `--color-sea`, one tier up: _"an Owner who sets `--color-sea` to purple gets the identical lie."_ The rule was applied to tier 2 and not to the thing tier 1 is keyed by.
- **CSS already spells it.** `tokens.css` sets `color-scheme: light` inside the `solar` block and `color-scheme: dark` inside `astral`. The platform's own vocabulary sits one line below ours, disagreeing.
- **Nothing offers the names as what they are.** Hexly has _two_ palettes and no way to pick one: `RADIUS_PRESETS` offers three corner sets and `FONT_PAIRINGS` offers a typeface pairing, but the largest identity lever in the system — the eleven tier-1 values ADR-0075 built precisely so that re-theming is "a 12-value job per ColorScheme instead of 35" — opens as a grid of eleven colour wells and no starting point but Hexly's own.

The scale is the reason this is worth doing now and would not be worth doing later: **961 mentions across the living surface**, of which 228 are a generated snapshot table. Every one of them is cheaper to move before the theming layer has downstream consumers than after.

## Considered Options

- **Keep `solar`/`astral` and accept the gloss.** Rejected. It is the status quo, and the status quo is a schema field whose name will be wrong for most Worlds that ever use it.
- **Rename the concept too** — `Appearance`, `Mode`, `Contrast Mode`. Rejected: "ColorScheme" was never the confusing part, and it is the word CSS itself uses. A second rename would cost five ADRs and CONTEXT.md and buy nothing.
- **Ship `light`/`dark` and keep `solar`/`astral` as accepted aliases on read.** Rejected. It costs no migration and it never ends: both vocabularies stay alive in the schema forever, which is the thing this decision exists to stop.
- **Presets as light/dark _pairs_ — one named identity, two faces.** Rejected, and it was close. It honours ADR-0006's binding constraint most directly and Hexly's own pair is already named that way. But it would be the **only** place in the system that couples the two Palettes: `worldThemeSchema` stores them independently, the editor edits them independently, and `overrides` keys them independently. Coupling them in the picker alone would mean the one surface where an Owner meets the concept disagrees with every other surface about what a Palette is.
- **Presets carrying only the eleven tier-1 values.** Rejected because of a named literal — see below.
- **Deriving `--color-canvas-glow` instead of stating it, so the exception disappears.** Not rejected on the merits; deferred as out of scope. ADR-0075 rejected it on the argument that a formula fitting _two_ targets is information-free, and six Palettes is precisely the extra data that would make such a fit falsifiable. That is a derivation-fitting spike, not a cleanup, and it should be taken with the measurement discipline `spike-token-derivation.md` used rather than folded in here.

## Decision

- **`ColorScheme` is `'light' | 'dark'`.** The concept name, `ColorSchemeService`, `data-color-scheme`, and the `colorScheme` Preference field are unchanged. The user-facing toggle drops to plain "Light" and "Dark" and stops glossing itself.
- **Solar and Astral name Palette Presets, and nothing else.** Any use of them as the day/night axis becomes light/dark — including `--palette-polarity`'s comment, which read `+1 Solar / −1 Astral`.
- **A Palette Preset is per-ColorScheme**, and carries what the editor permits for one ColorScheme: the **eleven tier-1 values, plus that ColorScheme's tier-2 overrides**. Radii and the font pairing are ColorScheme-independent — a light Preset carrying a radius set would mean an Owner's corners change when a reader toggles to dark — so they stay outside a Preset and keep their own pickers. This needs no schema change: a Preset writes into `light`/`dark` and `overrides[scheme]`, both of which already exist.
- **A Preset carries its own named literals.** Each dark Preset states its own `--color-canvas-glow`. This is not decoration: the stylesheet keys off `[data-color-scheme]`, and a stored Theme is values with **no Preset id**, so the stylesheet can never know which Preset is active — the `overrides` field is the only mechanism that can carry a per-Preset literal at all. Without it a warm-charcoal Preset inherits Astral's indigo starlight, which is ADR-0075's named-literal exception failing in exactly the way it was written to avoid.
- **Picking writes values and remembers nothing.** No Preset id enters stored data; which Preset a Palette _is_ is derived by comparison. This is `RADIUS_PRESETS`' own rule, for its own reason — _"stored as their values and never as an id, so renaming one here is no migration against stored Themes"_ — and it keeps a Preset a starting point an Owner then edits rather than a link they are bound to.
- **One table is the source of truth, and `tokens.css` is generated from it.** `PALETTE_PRESETS` lives in `libs/domain` (the server reads it, below), and `pnpm tokens:generate` writes the tier-1 blocks of `tokens.css` from the default Light and Dark entries, committed output with a drift spec — the same route the `@property` block and `index.html`'s allowlist already take. This is ADR-0075's own argument reapplied: it insisted Hexly's own schemes go through the derivation because _"a derivation exercised only by other people's themes is a code path nobody on this project ever looks at."_ A Preset table exercised only by other people's Palettes is the same trap.
- **Six Presets: three per ColorScheme, one neutral and one flavoured beside Hexly's own.** Light — **Solar** (default), **Vellum** (cool neutral paper, slate ink), **Herbarium** (pale sage, deep forest ink, brass). Dark — **Astral** (default), **Obsidian** (neutral near-black, cool ink, cyan), **Ember** (warm charcoal, ash ink, forge-orange). The split answers the two things an Owner actually arrives wanting: out of Hexly's sepia personality into something plain, or into a _different_ personality.
- **The accents are fitted, not picked.** The eight `--color-tone-*` are hue rotations off `--color-accent` (ADR-0075), so each Preset rotates the whole categorical set, and ADR-0076 already warned that the danger/success separation "was computed against Hexly's accent and does not automatically hold for theirs." An e2e spec therefore asserts **`themeWarnings()` returns nothing for all six**, collision checks included. Shipping a Preset that trips the warning we hand Owners is the worst possible introduction to that warning; where a Preset cannot pass, its anchors move, exactly as Solar's accent was already darkened for the same report.
- **Stored World Themes go to `version: 2`**, with a data-only SQL migration rewriting `$.solar`→`$.light` and `$.astral`→`$.dark` at the top level and under `$.overrides`. The version field exists for this: an old client meeting a migrated World gets ADR-0076's explicit refusal rather than a validation error about a missing `solar`. The roaming Preference is migrated in the same breath, by an `UPDATE` that is the exact mirror of migration `0029_color_scheme_preference.sql` — which took `theme: light|dark` **to** `colorScheme: solar|astral`. Half of it is being undone; the half worth keeping, the `colorScheme` key, stays.
- **Both localStorage surfaces lapse.** A stale `hexly-color-scheme: 'astral'` no longer matches and falls through to the OS preference; a stale per-World theme cache yields no declarations and paints unthemed for one frame. Both self-heal on first load and rewrite themselves, a signed-in reader is reasserted by the migrated roaming Preference, and no compatibility branch enters the pre-paint script in `index.html` — the code least able to carry one and least able to ever shed it.
- **`hexly.yml` may name a Preset**, per ColorScheme, either bare or as a Preset plus overriding anchors. Ids are safe here and unsafe in stored data for a reason that is not taste: instance config is re-read and validated **at boot**, and ADR-0076 already made the theme loader report every offending key rather than the first — so an id that no longer exists fails at startup with a message naming it, where a stored id would fail silently years later. It is also the difference between a config an operator uses and eleven hexes they paste.
- **`[data-color-scheme]` narrows back to `:root`.** With a Preset table in TypeScript, `hexlyPalette()` becomes a lookup, and it was the **last reader** of ADR-0076's widened selector: `measure.ts` re-dresses the document root (that ADR's own §5.3 amendment), `theme-overrides.component.ts` explicitly does not re-dress a subtree, and `map-canvas.component.ts`'s `:host-context()` matches at the root regardless. A widened selector with no reader is a documented capability that will be maintained and never exercised.
- **ADRs are history; living documents are rewritten.** ADR-0006, ADR-0075, ADR-0076 and the two spike documents keep their prose and gain amendment notes: they record what was decided _then_, and a decision record edited to agree with a later decision stops being evidence of anything. The spikes especially — "all 35 fitted tokens land at ΔE00 ≤ 2.91 in both schemes" is a claim about Solar and Astral specifically, and renaming its subjects would make it look like a claim about the new Presets, which it is not. Everything describing the system _now_ is rewritten: CONTEXT.md, `identity.md`, `world-theme-spec.md`, README, code comments, i18n.
- **Preset names are proper nouns and are not translated**, each with a translated one-line description. This is already the house rule, visible in the catalogs: `RADIUS_PRESETS`' names _are_ translated ("Sharp" → "Nets") because they are descriptive adjectives, while the `codex` font pairing is byte-identical in `en.json` and `fr.json` with only its description localised.

## Consequences

- **An Owner can now pick a mismatched pair in two clicks** — Solar's ivory by day and Ember's forge-orange by night. They could always author that, and ADR-0076 is explicit that this is a creative tool that warns rather than blocks; what changes is that the product now _offers_ it. The coherence ADR-0006 describes is Hexly's own, held by the default pair, and is no longer a property the system enforces on Worlds.
- **`--color-terrain-mountain` stays broken for non-indigo dark Presets.** It is the hex map's tier-3 named literal (ADR-0075's #369 amendment), violet by night, and `OVERRIDABLE_TOKENS` deliberately excludes tier 3 because a plugin's private vocabulary is not the design system's to theme. Reaching it means opening tier 3 to World Themes, which is a feature and not a fix. The gap already existed for any Owner authoring a custom dark Palette; Presets make it ours and visible on first click.
- **Picking a Preset marks an override row as overridden**, because it genuinely is one. An Owner can clear it back to derived. Honest, and a wrinkle the editor's copy has to name rather than hide.
- **Preset ids become a compatibility surface in `hexly.yml` only.** Renaming one is a breaking change for operators' config and a non-event for every stored World Theme — which is the whole point of the values-not-ids rule, and the reason the exception is confined to the file that fails loudly at boot.
- **The narrowed selector retires a fragile ordering constraint.** `hexmap-tokens.css` carries a comment explaining that its block must sit _above_ the dark block because both are specificity (0,1,0). With `:root` prefixes the dark block simply outranks the light one and source order stops mattering — a class of bug that could only be caught by looking.
- **`design-tokens.table.json` stays at one Palette per ColorScheme.** It exists to catch _derivation_ drift, and every expression is exercised by any one Palette; tripling it would triple the churn on every formula change for no additional signal. The six Presets are covered by the contrast gate instead, which is the property that actually differs between them.
- **Adding a seventh Preset is a table entry plus a passing contrast test**, and cannot be done without one. That is the intended shape: the gate is what makes "readily available" mean "we stand behind it."

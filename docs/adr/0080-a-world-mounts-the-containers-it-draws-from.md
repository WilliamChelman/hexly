# A World Mounts the Containers it draws from

Music the whole table shares — a "calm town" playlist, an "epic combat" one — belongs to no single **World**, and neither does a shelf of fantasy art a **Board** borrows from while an idea is still forming, nor a monster glanced at mid-brainstorm. ADR-0079's one door is **Adoption**, a copy: five Worlds then hold five copies of a playlist that is curated over time, over five copies of the same bytes, because content-addressed assets dedup per **Container** (ADR-0034). We relax the seal along exactly one line — **a link target must be in the linking Container or one it Mounts**. A **Mount** is a Container a World draws from, declared by a World Owner. **Containment is untouched** (one Entity, one Container), the seal's _read-only_ half is untouched, and every container-scoped listing — the **Entity Browser**, **Facets**, counts, Dashboard recents, the **World Graph** — is untouched, because a Mount widens what a World may _point at_, never what it _holds_.

The seal's no-link half was in any case already open at its own sanctioned door. **Adoption** copies an entry's values verbatim, so a Marauder Goblin adopted out of a pack arrives in a World carrying its outbound links to the Queen Goblin and the Green Skins faction — cross-container links, minted by the one act ADR-0079 endorsed, asserted by a passing test. ADR-0079 names the resulting condition in its own rejected-options list ("it splits a world's references between 'points at the shelf' and 'points at my copy'") without noticing that Adoption produces it. This ADR does not open that door; it scopes it.

**Mounting is per-World and explicit**, because the noise it exists to prevent is a picker offering five Worlds' worth of goblin kings, and an installed bestiary is that problem an order of magnitude larger. Nothing is auto-mounted. What widens is the **Link-target read** — the `@` picker, the **Entity Link** Field picker, the Board **Embed** picker — plus the asset and Board image pickers, which are not link pickers at all (a Board **Image** is a capability URL, decor by construction, ADR-0069) and would otherwise be the one surface the rule missed. Browsing what a World draws from is a surface of its own: ADR-0079's **Compendium browse** generalises into the **Library**, faceted by Container, with installed packs one kind of entry among the Shelves.

**A Mount cascades read, and you may only mount a Container you Own.** Reachability gains one disjunct beside the two it has — _the caller reaches C, or reaches some World W that Mounts C_ — and the Own-only rule is what makes that safe rather than an escalation: a Mount can only ever republish content you already control. Mounts do not chain and cycles are therefore harmless. The cascade reaches anonymous **World Public Link** holders too, which obliges the **Compendium page** to be anonymously reachable, so a pack's terms are never behind a wall its content is not.

**A shelf is a World.** Collaboration is the World's alone (ADR-0078), so anything authored needs the one kind that answers "who may write here?"; `worlds.kind` (`campaign | shelf`) is a label the **World Index** groups by and that no read ever filters on. That is not ADR-0078's rejected flag: run its test and _nothing_ needs excluding — a Shelf wants members, a Dashboard, a Theme, the Switcher, and to be mountable, exactly as a campaign does.

## Considered Options

- **Whole-container replication** — mirror a Container's Entities into each World and live-sync them, riding `entityImportSource`'s `(container, importer, sourceId)` upsert. The strongest rejected option, and the only one that changes no rule: reachability, containment, the graph, References and the Vault export all stay as they are, and closure over the copy means no cross-container link ever exists. Rejected on granularity above all — a 2000-entry bestiary mirrored into a World floods the Browser, Facets, counts and Graph, and the only cure is a "hide what came from a mirror" predicate on six read paths, which is `hiddenFromDefaultListing` and "has an **Import Source**" wearing a third hat, both rejected by name in ADR-0079. It also cannot serve packs, so it would ship beside Mounts rather than instead of them; it multiplies bytes rather than dividing them (per-Container CAS); and because `entities.id` is a global primary key a replica cannot keep its source's id, so every intra-set link must be rewritten locally on every sync, in every replicating World — the point where a copy becomes a sync engine, with write amplification, delete propagation and a conflict policy behind it.
- **Per-entity replication — Adoption with an origin record and a re-sync.** A tenth the machinery and no granularity problem, but it produces the hybrid the whole design is trying to avoid: adopt the Marauder, adopt the Queen separately, and the Marauder still points at the pack's Queen while your own sits unreferenced. Deferred as a decision, not rejected — it is ADR-0079's own deferred `hexly.adoptedFrom` and re-sync, and it composes with Mounts rather than competing.
- **Multi-container membership** — an Entity in N Containers. Rejected hardest: `entities.container_id` is _why_ six World-scoped reads exclude foreign content with no predicate anywhere. A join table gives every one of them a rule to remember, unwinding ADR-0078 and ADR-0079's central purchase weeks after making it.
- **Free cross-container linking, scoped by ranking alone.** Rejected by the problem statement: five Worlds of goblin kings in one picker is the condition, not the fix. Mounting makes the scope declared rather than scanned.
- **A Mount as pure discovery scope, granting nothing.** Cheapest, and leaves ADR-0037 entirely alone. Rejected: a shared shelf whose membership must be re-synced by hand against every campaign that draws on it is the manual chore this ADR exists to delete, and the failure is invisible to the person who caused it — the Owner's own view is fine while every player's Board shows dangling art.
- **Granting read per-link** — only entities actually pointed at become readable. The precise answer and the wrong engine: it makes "may I read this?" a question about _what points at it_, turning ADR-0037's standing-per-(caller, container) into a per-entity, graph-derived resolution.
- **A third Container kind for shelves, with Collaboration generalised to Containers.** Repointing `worldMembers`/`worldLinks` at `containers.id` and dispatching reachability per kind is coherent — ADR-0078's "two irreconcilable answers" objection is answerable, the answer being a kind dispatch. Rejected for now on what it costs: the by-construction guarantee that a Compendium _cannot_ hold members becomes a rule someone must remember instead of a schema that cannot express it, paid to buy per-group pack access that nothing needs yet. Still one rename away, exactly as ADR-0078 priced it.
- **Transitive Mounts.** Rejected: the set of things your pickers offer, and the set of people who can read your content, would both change when someone else edits _their_ Mount list; it reopens cycles and makes the reachability join recursive, forfeiting the single bounded join that made Mounts cheaper than replication. The price is duplication in Mount lists — two or three rows, edited by hand.
- **Auto-mounting installed Compendiums** (always, or on World creation). Rejected: it makes the noisiest Container the one thing a campaign cannot switch off, and the create-time variant adds invisible history for the same experience.
- **Refusing to unmount or delete a Container that is linked into.** Rejected: a destructive act another user's configuration can veto is worse than a dangling link, and ADR-0079's own posture is that discovery holds the line, not invariants.

## Consequences

- **ADR-0037 is un-cemented.** Reachability grows a second, indirect path, and for the first time reading a Container depends on another Container's configuration. Bounded — one `EXISTS`, one hop — but it is a new shape.
- **A Compendium is mountable by anyone, so a pack's content can reach anonymous readers.** The Own-only rule bounds every other Mount, and a **Compendium** has no Owner for it to name: it is Instance-wide, so any caller who may create a World may mount every installed pack. Beside a cascade that deliberately reaches **World Public Link** holders, that is a redistribution path — mount a pack, mint a link, and licensed content stands in front of unauthenticated readers, with no operator control over which packs may be republished and no per-pack opt-out. Accepted as it stands, weighed rather than missed, and left for a later decision.
- **Dangling links become a routine condition rather than a hazard designed out.** Unmount, Container delete and pack removal each produce them, and each answers with a count of the links and Worlds affected — a number and a confirm, on ADR-0074's prompt-rather-than-gate posture and ADR-0065's usage-aware delete. Unmount is the one worth wording carefully: the links keep working for the Owner who unmounted and stop working for everyone reading through that World.
- **A Mount scopes what may be newly pointed at and what readers inherit; it does not gate links that already exist**, nor what they mean. The asset-URL harvest therefore resolves `(containerId, hash)` from the URL itself rather than through the Mount set — otherwise unmounting would dissolve edges at the next write while the images kept rendering, and References would quietly disagree with the page. This also repairs a pre-existing leak: a cross-World asset URL rendered (the byte route is unauthenticated and takes the container from the path) while harvesting to nothing, so its bytes crossed the wall and its meaning did not.
- **Foreign targets appear as terminal nodes** in the **World Graph** and the **Local Graph**, marked and never expanded. Never-expanded is load-bearing: without it, depth 2 from a tavern note reaches a shelf image and then every other World that uses it, leaking the shape of other campaigns into this one's Panel. Drowning is not a risk — only what was actually linked appears, and most of it is **Decor Links**, already subdued behind ADR-0069's reveal.
- **Backlinks cross the boundary.** A shelf Entity's References list the Worlds using it, access-filtered as backlinks already are — the surface that answers "may I delete this image?", without which a shelf is where things go to become un-auditable.
- **A World is no longer self-contained on export.** The Vault export flattens the _bytes_ a document references from a mounted Container, because a broken image reads as data loss while a wikilink to a missing note reads as a stub — and an **Unresolved Link** is promotable by design (ADR-0073). Export → import is consequently not identity: you get copies where you had references. Full flattening is deferred and filed with "publish a World as a Compendium", which is the same problem and deserves one answer.
- **The asset pickers' own listing goes; its Contributor gate moves onto the shared read.** Once they ask the Mount-aware **Link-target read**, `GET /worlds/:id/assets(/facets)` has no question left to answer — but the reasoning that gated it holds wherever it is asked: a listed capability URL is fetchable through the guard-less byte route (ADR-0034), so a listing _is_ the bytes and a World **Viewer** must not enumerate art. So a link-target read offers **Assets** only to a caller who may create Entities in the read's own scope, and the full-resolution URL a summary now carries beside the thumbnail rides the same standing. The standing is the _scope's_, not the row's, which is what keeps a mounted Shelf's art offered to a Contributor of the mounting World — nobody holds a role on a Shelf they merely draw from. A Viewer keeps the **Asset Browser** unchanged, tiles and all: a container-scoped browse of what this World holds is no editing surface, and the address was never withheld from a reader of the Asset's own page.

- **Following a link can leave your World.** Entity URLs are World-scoped (ADR-0028), so clicking through a mounted Embed lands at the content's own Container. Honest, and a wart you will feel.
- **The World Index becomes a mixed list**, grouped by `worlds.kind`. A World is now "the Container a user authors into", with campaign-or-setting demoted from the definition to the typical case.
- **Adoption stops being the only door and becomes the _fork_ door** — you adopt when you mean to change the thing. Its unchanged behaviour of copying links verbatim is now legal rather than a hole, and adopting from an unmounted Compendium leaves links a signed-in caller can follow and an anonymous one cannot. Adoption deliberately does not auto-mount: a silent edit to a World's reachability configuration is worse than a link one class of reader cannot follow.
- **The nav rail reads Entities beside Library**, and ADR-0079's retirement of "Library" is completed rather than reversed — it was retired for leaning _reference_, and this is the reference surface. Each word ends up naming the thing it always leaned toward.
- **A World with no Mounts behaves exactly like ADR-0079's sealed model**, which is what lets one predicate serve both and leaves nothing to reverse.
